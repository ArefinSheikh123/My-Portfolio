Man-Portable EOD ROV

A compact, field-deployable robot. Custom drive systems, carbon-fiber structures, and mechanical components built in-house.

CASE STUDY / 01—05

01 / THE PROJECT

From concept to working hardware.

A man-portable Explosive Ordnance Disposal robot calls for a careful balance of compact packaging, structural strength, and reliable motion. I engineered and manufactured its mechanical components in-house, from the carbon-fiber frame to the custom drive system.

The drive system centers on a globoidal worm gearbox designed for high torque in a compact envelope. Tooth geometry, wear resistance, and manufacturing constraints informed the mechanism design.

Wet layup and vacuum bagging made lightweight composite structures possible within the local fabrication workflow. Designing the mechanism and the manufacturing process together helped turn CAD assemblies into functional hardware.

PROJECT STATUS / IN PROGRESS

A Compact, Man-Portable Remotely Operated Vehicle for Hazardous-Environment Inspection and Manipulation

The platform is designed for hazardous-environment inspection and manipulation in confined or uneven spaces. A dual-mode tracked drive and independently controlled flippers support terrain adaptation, while a detachable telescoping arm accepts different tools for remote handling.

Operators use body, gripper and pan-tilt-zoom camera views to monitor the work area. Modular tool mounting, precise manipulation and intelligent motion control support work around hazardous or unstable materials. The portable controller is specified with a daylight-readable screen, wired and wireless communication, two-way audio and overload protection. Shock absorption and weather-resistant enclosures are part of the field-use brief.

PROJECT OBJECTIVE

To develop a ROV which is capable of precise manipulation, terrain adaptability, and reliable operation in critical missions including industrial maintenance work, inspection, and rescue operations.
